How accurate must a scan be?

93 simulated Panda picks. The robot always plans with the nominal scan; the simulated "real" mug differs. A: collision geometry decides it. One hull or a bounding box (library variants) fails at every friction. B: doubling the mass breaks the grip, so weigh your object. C: size errors within ±5% are tolerated even with ±8 mm grasp error; the ColorChecker's ≤1.4% scale error is well inside. A real mug 10–15% smaller than the scan makes the fingers miss the rim. Grip force ~9 N (a real Panda hand: up to 70 N).

Train on synthetic images, test on real photos

A Faster R-CNN detector trained only on Isaac Sim renders of our scanned assets, run on the 67 real Buddha photos it never saw. Green: the Buddha. Red: false detections. Trained without distractors, the detector calls markers and lab clutter "mugs".

Learn

Photogrammetry with COLMAP · photos → points → mesh

Structure from motion matches features across photos and solves every camera's position. Multi-view stereo then estimates depth per pixel and fuses millions of colored points. A Poisson solve turns the points into a closed surface.

reconstruct.py · table plane → Z-up; object kept as the densest footprint above the table

Accuracy, completeness, F-score · how reconstruction is graded

Accuracy: how far the built surface is from the truth. Completeness: how much of the truth was captured. F-score at τ combines both: the share of each surface within τ (2 mm here) of the other. These are the standard metrics of the DTU and Tanks & Temples benchmarks.

evaluate.py · yaw search + ICP alignment, then point-to-surface distances

Color calibration · physically based calibration, for scans

A 3×3 matrix maps the camera's colors of known chart patches to their true values in linear light; it's then applied to the scan's albedo. ΔE2000 ≈ 1 is just noticeable.

color.py

Metric scale from the chart · no ruler needed

Photogrammetry alone can't tell a mug from a giant mug: its scale is arbitrary. The ColorChecker's patches are a known distance apart (16 mm on the Mini), so the pipeline finds the chart in many photos, triangulates its 24 patch centers with the solved cameras, and reads off meters per unit. RANSAC drops misdetections. On the benchmark: 0.35–1.43% error.

chart.py · scan2usd scan --chart-pitch-mm 16

Convex decomposition · why the grasp works

Physics engines collide convex shapes quickly. One hull around a mug fills its cavity; CoACD splits it into pieces that keep the opening, so fingers and objects can go inside.

physics.py

Mass for real, holey scans · voxel fill

Mass needs a closed solid. Scans often have holes, so the pipeline voxelizes the surface, closes small gaps, and fills enclosed space; open cavities (bowls, cups) stay empty. Example: bowl 322 g (hull estimate) → 192 g.

physics.voxel_solid

Articulations in USD · lids, doors, laptops

Each part is its own rigid body; a UsdPhysics.RevoluteJoint joins them with a hinge axis and angle limits, under an ArticulationRootAPI so PhysX solves them as one mechanism.

articulation.py · scan2usd articulate

USD composition: payloads & variants · one library, many configurations

USD builds a scene from layers. A payload is a reference that loads on demand, so a 1,000-asset library opens instantly and loads only what a scene needs. A variant set is a named switch with alternative opinions inside, here collision and friction. Variants are stronger than payloads (the "LIVRPS" strength order), so the library can hide an asset's colliders and add simpler ones without editing the asset file. The same idea drives LODs and material looks in film and game pipelines.

library.py · scan2usd library · catalog

Synthetic data & sim-to-real · Replicator, domain randomization

Robots learn perception from labeled images. Simulators render perfect labels for free. The catch is the sim-to-real gap: a model that's perfect on renders can still fail on photos. Domain randomization varies everything that shouldn't matter (lights, colors, camera, junk objects) so the model learns only what does. Here, distractors cut false positives on real photos by 84%.

Lessons so far

  • Texture decides reconstruction. A plain dark glaze and a plain white mug interior both failed to reconstruct. That shows up in the benchmark as holes or a sealed cup (volume +191%).
  • Validators can't catch everything. A wrong object pick (a 38 cm ring) still passed every plausibility check; scoring against ground truth caught it.
  • Clustering by distance breaks on sparse surfaces. Picking the object by its 2D footprint on the table worked where 3D distance clustering failed.
  • Units bite. MuJoCo angles default to degrees; a radian limit produced a 1.8° hinge.
  • Contacts need consistent stiffness. Soft default bin walls let objects sink 8 mm; matching the assets' stiffness gave 0.08 mm.
  • Fast + thin = tunneling. At PhysX's default 60 Hz, a dropped mug moved 4.5 cm per step and passed through a 2 cm table; a thicker slab and 240 Hz fixed it.
  • Versions matter. Isaac Sim 5.1's renderer crashed on this driver; 6.0 works.
  • Robot models need realistic actuators. The Panda's stock gripper servo squeezes ~0.5 N on a thin cup wall, so the mug slipped; a real Panda hand reaches 70 N, and a stiffer servo (~9 N) made the pick work.
  • Test on real data, not just synthetic. The detector scored a perfect 1.00 on held-out renders and still made 4.3 false detections per real photo. Teaching it what to ignore (unlabeled distractors) fixed most of it.
  • Labels can be wrong too. Replicator's instance masks sometimes dropped or shrank an object; the converter now trusts the box annotator and keeps a mask only when it agrees.
  • Know your camera model. Back-projected depth was 3 cm off because MuJoCo's scene stores two stereo eye cameras; a mono render uses their average. One line fixed it.
  • Some things can't be observed. With the handle hidden from both cameras, the mug's yaw has no answer. Design the action not to need it: the rim grasp works at any yaw.

Capture guide

Shoot

  • Start easy: a matte, textured object (wooden toy, sneaker, speckled mug).
  • 60–150 photos: walk around it in 3 rings with 60–80% overlap; keep object and lights still; lock exposure, white balance and focus; no flash.
  • Put a newspaper under the object and the ColorChecker beside it in a few frames, plus one close-up.
  • The Xbox controller after that: dark gloss is the hardest case (see the lesson above).

Measure, then run

  • Weigh it (g). A ruler measurement (mm) is optional if the ColorChecker is in the photos.
  • scan2usd scan photos\ -o out\ --chart-pitch-mm 16 --mass 0.36 --material ceramic (photos → USD in one step; measure your chart's patch spacing once)
  • Put files in data\raw\<object>\ (git-ignored).
