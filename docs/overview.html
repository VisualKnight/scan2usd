How the answer key is made

  • Scale from two tape measurements; the combined scan agrees within 1.2%.
  • Two controller passes merged by similarity ICP (0.5 mm RMSE).
  • Ground truth: both assets registered into the combined scan (holder 2.8 mm, controller 1.0 mm RMSE), so the settle error is an upper bound.
  • Leg gap: Poisson filled in the space between the figure's legs; it's carved back out where the photos see background through it (before/after).

How accurate must a scan be?

93 simulated Panda picks, planned on the nominal scan while the “real” mug differs. A: collision geometry decides it; one hull or a bounding box fails at every friction. B: doubling the mass breaks the grip, so weigh the object. C: ±5% size error is tolerated; the ColorChecker's ≤1.43% is well inside.

Rendering: Isaac Sim and Unreal Engine

Same USD files in Unreal Engine 5.8: Lumen + MegaLights

One USD stage: the scans by reference, a wood table and 80 shadow-casting fairy lights, imported unchanged. Lumen gives the bounce light, MegaLights a soft shadow for every bulb. Scripted build and render. 10-second orbit.

What MegaLights buys: 8.8 vs 14.8 ms GPU per frame

Median GPU frame at 1080p on an RTX 4090. MegaLights samples the lights stochastically instead of drawing a shadow map per light: about 40% less GPU time, and slightly closer to Unreal's path tracer (PSNR 24.8 vs 23.3 dB).

Build

Assets that know what they don't know

Scan-to-sim tools usually output one confident mesh. These assets also store what the camera actually saw, what was guessed, how to hold them, and how they really look: all inside the USD file, where any simulator can read it.

5 / 6true masses inside the stored interval; the single nominal value is within 10% for only 2 / 6
756 re-testsof the best Shadow Hand and Panda grasps under perturbation, stored in the benchmark assets; Isaac Sim's Franka reads the best one and picks
0.49 → 0.95bowl F-score when the asset's stored "carved" hypothesis assumes the cavity no camera saw into is empty

Uncertainty in the asset (Scan2UsdUncertaintyAPI)

Every vertex is labeled observed, inferred or sealed. Mass, scale and friction are stored as intervals, plus a carved hypothesis that assumes unseen cavities are empty (bowl F-score 0.49 → 0.95).

Grasps in the asset (Scan2UsdGraspAPI)

Grasp poses with their tested success rate per gripper and collision variant. Isaac Sim's Franka reads the best one and picks (5.5 mm). Randomizing physics within the stored intervals predicts real grasp success better than the nominal asset (Brier 0.12 → 0.05).

Hybrid asset: Gaussian splat for looks, hulls for physics

A splat trained on the same photos, masked by the mesh's silhouettes, rides on the rigid body as a UsdVol.ParticleField3DGaussianSplat. On 8 real photos it never saw: PSNR 19.7 → 27.0 dB.

Splats ride on PhysX

Splat Buddha and mugs fall on their convex hulls in Isaac Sim RTX; a hidden copy of the mesh casts their shadows. Video · Franka picks a splat mug.

Notes

Learn

Photogrammetry with COLMAP · photos → points → mesh

Structure from motion matches features across photos and solves every camera's position. Multi-view stereo then estimates depth per pixel and fuses millions of colored points. A Poisson solve turns the points into a closed surface.

reconstruct.py · table plane → Z-up; object kept as the densest footprint above the table

Accuracy, completeness, F-score · how reconstruction is graded

Accuracy: how far the built surface is from the truth. Completeness: how much of the truth was captured. F-score at τ combines both: the share of each surface within τ (2 mm here) of the other. These are the standard metrics of the DTU and Tanks & Temples benchmarks.

evaluate.py · yaw search + ICP alignment, then point-to-surface distances

Normal maps & PBR · the game-art pipeline, for scans

Real-time engines render a light mesh and fake the fine detail: a normal map stores, per texel, how the dense surface tilts relative to the low-poly one (in the "tangent space" of its UV layout). PBR materials describe surfaces physically (base color, roughness, metalness, index of refraction, a clearcoat layer for glazes), so they look right under any light. A path tracer then simulates the light itself. For robotics, the same assets give physics a light collision mesh and give cameras realistic images.

bake.bake_normal_map · usd_io.LOOKS · scripts/isaac_pathtrace.py

Color calibration · physically based calibration, for scans

A 3×3 matrix maps the camera's colors of known chart patches to their true values in linear light; it's then applied to the scan's albedo. ΔE2000 ≈ 1 is just noticeable.

color.py

Metric scale from the chart · no ruler needed

Photogrammetry alone can't tell a mug from a giant mug: its scale is arbitrary. The ColorChecker's patches are a known distance apart (16 mm on the Mini), so the pipeline finds the chart in many photos, triangulates its 24 patch centers with the solved cameras, and reads off meters per unit. RANSAC drops misdetections. On the benchmark: 0.35–1.43% error.

chart.py · scan2usd scan --chart-pitch-mm 16

Convex decomposition · why the grasp works

Physics engines collide convex shapes quickly. One hull around a mug fills its cavity; CoACD splits it into pieces that keep the opening, so fingers and objects can go inside.

physics.py

Mass for real, holey scans · voxel fill

Mass needs a closed solid. Scans often have holes, so the pipeline voxelizes the surface, closes small gaps, and fills enclosed space; open cavities (bowls, cups) stay empty, as long as the reconstruction left them open (see the benchmark's sealed mug). Example: bowl 322 g (hull estimate) → 192 g.

physics.voxel_solid

Articulations in USD · lids, doors, laptops

Each part is its own rigid body; a UsdPhysics.RevoluteJoint joins them with a hinge axis and angle limits, under an ArticulationRootAPI so PhysX solves them as one mechanism.

articulation.py · scan2usd articulate

USD composition: payloads & variants · one library, many configurations

USD builds a scene from layers. A payload is a reference that loads on demand, so a 1,000-asset library opens instantly and loads only what a scene needs. A variant set is a named switch with alternative opinions inside, here collision and friction. Variants are stronger than payloads (the "LIVRPS" strength order), so the library can hide an asset's colliders and add simpler ones without editing the asset file. The same idea drives LODs and material looks in film and game pipelines.

library.py · scan2usd library · catalog

Synthetic data & sim-to-real · Replicator, domain randomization

Robots learn perception from labeled images. Simulators render perfect labels for free. The catch is the sim-to-real gap: a model that's perfect on renders can still fail on photos. Domain randomization varies everything that shouldn't matter (lights, colors, camera, junk objects) so the model learns only what does. Here, distractors cut false positives on real photos by 84%.

scripts/isaac_synthdata.py · scripts/train_detector.py

Gaussian splatting · a photo-real look from the same photos

Instead of a surface with a texture, a splat is a cloud of small, colored, semi-transparent 3D Gaussians, optimized until their renders match the photos. Each one stores its color as spherical harmonics, so it changes with the viewing angle (sheen, glaze). The result looks like the photos, but there's no surface, so a physics engine can't collide with it. OpenUSD 26.03 added a standard prim for them (ParticleField3DGaussianSplat). Here the mesh does the physics and also tells the splat trainer where the object is (alpha masks).

splat.py · scan2usd splat-data → Brush → scan2usd splat · scripts/isaac_splat.py

Uncertainty in an asset · intervals, not just values

A scan measures some things well (surface it saw from two angles), guesses others (a hole the mesher closed) and can't know some at all (the inside of a cup nobody photographed). Storing that with the asset lets a simulator randomize physics where the scan is unsure (domain randomization, but grounded in the measurement) and tells you which photos to take next. Custom properties live in a codeless USD schema: a plugin with only a plugInfo.json and a schema file, no compiled code, so Isaac Sim, usdview and Python all read it.

uncertainty.py · schemas.py · guide.py · scan2usd uncertainty | grasps | guide

Notes

Lessons

  1. Test on real data, not just synthetic. The detector scored a perfect 1.00 on held-out renders and still made 4.3 false detections per real photo. Unlabeled distractors in training cut that by 84%.
  2. Read the units, don't tune by eye. Depth of field was far too strong: USD lens values are in tenths of a scene unit, so on a meters stage “50” is a 5 m lens.
  3. Look at a cross-section before believing an explanation. The bowl's +82% volume looked like thick walls; slicing the solid showed an unseen underside filled into a pedestal. No line of sight, so carving can't fix it, but a stored "carved" hypothesis can.
  4. Robot models need realistic actuators. The stock Panda gripper squeezed a cup wall with 0.5–2 N and the mug slipped; a real Franka hand reaches 70 N.
  5. Don't teleport a hand. A mocap-driven forearm moves without velocity, and MuJoCo friction is velocity-based: 120 N of squeeze and the object stayed put. Welding a free forearm to the target fixed it.
  6. Visible isn't reconstructable. The capture guide aimed into the printed mug's white interior: in view, but nothing for stereo to match. Measuring the guide on three objects, not one, is what showed it.
  7. Let the mesh help the splat. Trained on raw photos, a splat spends its Gaussians on the background. Masking every photo with the mesh's silhouette raised held-out PSNR from 21.6 to 28.9 dB.
  8. Control at the physics rate. In Isaac Sim the hand kept dropping the controller: targets set once per 60 Hz frame moved it in jolts across 8 PhysX substeps. Setting them every substep took it from 0/4 to 12/12.
  9. Meshes need UVs, even for flat colors. In Unreal the hand rendered dark whatever its color: without UVs its tangents were degenerate, so even a flat normal map shaded it dark.
  10. A wide lens makes the nearest thing look big. The true-scale Shadow Hand looked oversized on MuJoCo's default 24 mm-equivalent view, because it's always nearest the camera. A 57 mm lens from farther back reads naturally.

Capture

Capture your own

Shoot

  • Start easy: a matte, textured object (wooden toy, sneaker, speckled mug). Dark, glossy or plain surfaces are the hardest; masking tape with pen marks helps inside plain cups.
  • 60–150 photos: walk around it in 3 rings with 60–80% overlap; keep object and lights still; lock exposure, white balance and focus; no flash.
  • Put a newspaper under the object and a ColorChecker beside it in a few frames, plus one close-up.

Measure, then run

  • Weigh it; --mass is in kilograms (360 g → --mass 0.36). A ruler measurement (mm) is optional if the ColorChecker is in the photos.
  • scan2usd scan photos\ -o out\ --chart-pitch-mm 16 --mass 0.36 --material ceramic (photos → USD in one step; measure your chart's patch spacing once)
  • scan2usd guide out\asset\<name>.usda --workspace out\colmap suggests extra shots.
